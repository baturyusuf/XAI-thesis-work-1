## Fixed-policy argument

Condition on an independent training/fitting sample B, fixed fitted score, fixed selector/threshold, population law, and all independently prespecified randomization rules. Treat score randomness as part of each **augmented base unit**. Suppose the calibration augmented units are iid and independent of B, with `(A_i, Lhat_i)` iid across units, `A_i` binary, `Lhat_i` bounded in `[0,1]`; dependence of acceptance and loss *within* a unit is allowed. Let `C=P(A=1)`, `K=sum_i A_i`. For `C>0`, `Rhat=E[Lhat|A=1]`. For any particular acceptance vector with k ones, independence across units implies the k accepted loss coordinates are independent and each has the conditional law `Lhat|A=1`. That product law does not depend on which vector with k ones occurred. Mixing vectors conditional on `K=k` therefore preserves the iid product law for k>0. This does **not** follow after selecting a new selector based on these calibration losses. `K~Bin(n,C)` under these assumptions.

A finite-sample bounded-loss super-uniform p-value for the one-sided null `Rhat>=alpha` applied to the k selected losses is super-uniform **conditional on each `K=k>0`**. At k=0 define p=1 and risk=None; no rejection and no meaningful conditional risk estimate. Mixing over k retains marginal super-uniformity. If C=0, K=0 almost surely and conditional population risk is undefined, not zero. Rejecting the inclusive unsafe null also controls the strict unsafe null `R>alpha` in the cited paper. The one-sided HB test is bounded-loss, not a binomial test for arbitrary fractional losses: `ceil(sum Lhat)` appears inside the *inequality's* binomial CDF, not as an exact count of Bernoulli errors. For binary losses only, the accepted count of errors actually follows `Bin(k,R)` and an exact tail can be used. Our earlier finite enumeration checked that special case, not the universal HB inequality.

To transfer the observed test to the intended latent risk, require `E[Lhat|base unit, fitted score, explanation, acceptance-relevant score randomness]=L_exact` (or a sufficient finer conditioning). Then by the tower property `E[A Lhat]=E[A L_exact]` and `E[Lhat|A=1]=E[L_exact|A=1]`; merely *marginal* unbiasedness is insufficient. Disjoint loss/score probes and independent B fitting are substantive requirements, not proven by IDs or guard flags. Multiple probes of one base unit contribute one bounded MC average, **not** additional iid n or K. Conditioning on a single global score-randomness draw that is reused across all calibration units would not yield the claimed iid augmented-unit argument without further conditioning/justification.

## Two controllers, familywise and coverage scope

For transformed LTT, `T_i=alpha+A_i*(Lhat_i-alpha)` lies in `[0,1]` and `E[T]-alpha=C*(R-alpha)` for C>0; it tests with n=512 whole base units. Accepted-count tests with random k. Neither theorem implies equal p-values or that one dominates in power. `calibrate_accepted_ltt` invokes the A3 guard and discards its risk decision; the subsequent accepted-count loop uses exact rational `k`, loss mean and fixed thresholds. A3 transformed risk remains unchanged. Neither implementation establishes the assumptions scientifically.

There are six declared score families, two controllers, and nine fixed thresholds per family. For **each** family/controller risk Bonferroni spends `delta_r=1/240`, or `1/2160` per threshold. Separate simultaneous coverage spends `delta_c=1/240` per family/controller, with `r=sqrt(log(9/delta_c)/(2*512))`, lower bound `max(0,k/512-r)` and required floor `1/20` (plus the implementation's conservative decision guard). Union over six times two times `(risk+coverage)` is `24/240=1/10`; dependence among shared calibration records/controllers does not break a union bound. The guarantee is: with probability at least 0.9 over independent calibration augmented units, **all** policies certified by these prespecified families/controllers with positive coverage lower bound have population accepted risk at most 1/5, conditional on independently fixed B/target/families and the assumptions above. It is not a guarantee that any policy will be selected, or a bound on realized evaluation-sample risk. Selection among already certified candidates is covered; fitting a score, threshold family/order, or policy *from the same calibration outcomes* and testing it as if fixed is not. The two selected policies share C and cannot be treated as independent replications.
