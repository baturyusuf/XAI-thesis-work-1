# H2 accepted-count independent audit plan (pre-handoff)

Status: mathematical preparation only; Agent4 has no immutable SHA/handoff. No Agent4 code or methods inspected or run. No real, private, hidden, or benchmark observations used. Do not treat these checks as validation of a native implementation or as final protocol/threshold choices.

## Target and proof obligations

Condition on a policy/selector, target law, risk tolerance 0 < alpha < 1, candidate family and testing order fixed independently of calibration. The base observations (A_i,L_i) are IID, A_i is binary, and 0 <= L_i <= 1 on acceptance; C=P(A=1)>0 and R=E[L|A=1]. K=sum A_i is random, K~Bin(n,C). Conditional on K=k>0, the k accepted losses have the IID accepted-population law (even if A and L are dependent within a unit). This is a distributional statement, not a claim that conditioning on post-hoc policy selection preserves IID. For binary L, E_i=sum A_i L_i | K=k ~ Bin(k,R); the one-sided null R>=alpha (including its boundary; the paper states R>alpha) has conservative p= P{Bin(k,alpha)<=E_i}. Monotonicity in R and the discrete probability-integral transform show P(p<=u|K=k)<=u for every k>0 and u in [0,1]. Set p=1 at K=0: no selective-risk estimate exists then. Summing over k proves unconditional superuniformity, including zero. C=0 makes conditional R undefined and must never be reported as risk zero or certified.

For fractional accepted losses, the error sum is not binomial. Use a finite-sample bounded-loss one-sided conditional test, e.g. p_H=exp[-2k max(alpha-mean(L_acc),0)^2] for k>0, p=1 for k=0; conditional Hoeffding suffices under IID and [0,1]. HB may be considered with its proved constants and rounding, not substituted with an exact binary tail. Unbounded losses require a different justified test; an asymptotic CLT is not a finite-sample guarantee.

As a separate valid estimand reduction, T=alpha+A(L-alpha) lies in [0,1] and E[T]-alpha=C(R-alpha) when C>0. A full-base bounded test of T uses n base units, tests the same population risk null, and need not have the same power as a conditional accepted-count test. At C=0, T=alpha but R is undefined: abstain. Compare on identical fixed policy families, base samples, risk and coverage budgets, loss definitions, and multiplicity corrections; report observed rejection/abstention frequencies and separate mathematical finite-sample conditional claims from empirical comparisons. Do not assert dominance from tiny enumerations.

## Family, data and coverage gates

For each predeclared candidate, obtain a valid marginal p-value. Apply Bonferroni over all candidates at delta_r/m, or pre-fix a single-start sequence and stop at the first non-rejection; same-calibration candidate picking/order fitting followed by a single unadjusted test is invalid. Multiple families/orders require an overall FWER procedure or preallocated risk budgets (sum <= delta_r). Choosing *among already certified* candidates is different from fitting/retesting candidates on calibration. No independence of candidate p-values is required for Bonferroni/fixed sequence.

Conditional unbiasedness of a bounded Monte Carlo loss must hold given each base observation AND the score/acceptance-relevant randomness: E[Lhat|base observation, acceptance and score-relevant randomness]=L_exact (or another explicitly justified target). Marginal unbiasedness is insufficient when noisy probes affect acceptance. Repeated probes from one base unit do not raise n or K. Clustered rows do not become IID by splitting or bootstrapping; the unit and estimand need justification.

Coverage C needs a separate positive lower bound for each predeclared policy. A one-sided simultaneous Hoeffding bound on n IID acceptance indicators is max(0, Chat-sqrt(log(m/delta_c)/(2n))) for m policies. Require lower >= a prespecified c_min>0 before certifying/using conditional risk. Risk and coverage error budgets are separate; joint failure <= delta_r+delta_c, plus other independently budgeted families as appropriate. For a fixed IID selector, C=0 implies K=0 almost surely (so K>0 has probability zero); seeing K>0 is incompatible with C=0 under that model, but does not establish a useful prespecified positive lower floor c_min or simultaneous confidence across policies. No final c_min, alpha or delta is set here.

## Standalone evidence and handoff checks

`tests/independent_validation/test_accepted_count_reference.py` uses stdlib `Fraction` and IID enumeration at n=6: four binary null laws (C=1/4 or 3/4; R=1/2 or 3/4, alpha=1/2) check conditional counts, binomial error law, conditional and unconditional attained-cutoff superuniformity, including K=0. A bounded fractional accepted law (L=0 or 1/2, alpha=1/4) checks conditional/unconditional Hoeffding superuniformity with exact probability weights and floating exponential comparisons. A prespecified complementary-two-candidate example at n=2 has both strict unsafe risks R=1/2>alpha=49/100. At delta=(1-alpha)^2=2601/10000, each fixed test rejects with probability 1/4<=delta, but choosing the smallest p on the same sample falsely rejects with probability 1/2>delta; Bonferroni at delta/2 rejects neither. Constants are synthetic demonstrations, not benchmark thresholds. See `research/validation/evidence/accepted_count_reference_results.json` for the executed command and counts.
