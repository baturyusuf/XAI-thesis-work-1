## Design and reporting limitations

- Held-out users are clustered units, not independent rating rows. Repeated OOF predictions for the same user and correlated inner/outer folds are not independent datasets or independent replications. User-resampled fixed-prediction bootstrap intervals condition on fitted models and chosen splits; they do not capture model-refit or population-of-sources uncertainty. Avoid per-fold or per-source significance claims, treating three sources as evidence of source-level significance, or extrapolating to new items/future ratings.
- Item overlap is high; estimates concern held-out users and their observed historical item choices conditional on the observed item pool. Ratings are selected observations, not a representative sample of all possible user-item pairs. Do not label this an unseen-item or prospective evaluation.
- Feature sets are deliberately sparse: 12 restaurant, 4 sushi and 21 MovieLens allowed fields. Item ID is excluded and several preference or content features are absent, so negative comparisons need not generalize to richer or personalized recommenders. Feature count does not itself establish how informative features are. Train-only preprocessing and user-grouped fitting are supported by inspected runner design; saved outputs alone do not independently prove model fit provenance.
- The freeze is prospective after earlier work, and three eligible task families do not satisfy the original four-task criterion. No final GO, source-level inference or independent confirmation follows from PASS. Retain exploratory framing, all nulls/reversals, selection counts, endpoint support and tradeoffs. Avoid interpreting a constraint imposed on validation as a guarantee on held-out endpoints.
