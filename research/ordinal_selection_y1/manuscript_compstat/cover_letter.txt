Dear Editors,

We submit the manuscript "Which metric should choose the model? Selection criteria, class weighting and endpoint recall in tabular ordinal classification" for consideration as an original research article in Computational Statistics.

Ordinal classifiers are usually tuned by maximizing a single validation criterion, most often the quadratically weighted kappa. The paper asks what that choice does to the extreme categories. On one fixed pool of proportional-odds and random-forest candidates, trained with and without class-balanced weights, seven selection criteria choose from the same saved inner cross-validation predictions. The study covers ten real tabular ordinal tasks from nine sources and four control tasks, all under grouped nested cross-validation, and a controlled simulation complements the real-data analysis. The main findings are:

- The selection criterion largely determines whether class weighting is adopted. Fixed-hyperparameter controls explain why: weighting nearly always increases MAE and reduces the worst class-wise error.
- The effect of class weighting, and of selection by average class-wise MAE (AMAE), on an endpoint category follows a simple rule: it helps endpoints whose share is below 1/K and tends to hurt those above it. A short proposition shows why for prior correction, and the rule explains why AMAE protects the endpoints in some tasks and harms them in others.
- A constant middle-category prediction attains a data-independent normalized AMAE of (K+1)/(4K) with zero endpoint recall. In four of the ten tasks this level was lower than the AMAE of every fitted candidate.
- An explicit endpoint-recall floor added to the kappa criterion protects the endpoints when it is feasible. No criterion was close to optimal on all evaluation metrics.
- A pre-specified fourth stage tested these claims with 24 candidates from three model families, including gradient boosting, and with a simulation designed for the 1/K rule. Six of the eight predictions were supported; the weighting effect changed sign exactly where the share of a category equals 1/K. The two failures are reported in full: the AMAE rule needs enough signal, and the recall floor failed once through its fallback.

We believe the work fits the journal's interest in computational methodology for statistical learning and in the assessment of statistical procedures. It continues a line of work published in the journal on how the evaluation metric changes model selection (de la Cruz Huayanay, Bazán and Russo, 2024, https://doi.org/10.1007/s00180-024-01539-5). The paper reports reversals, failed predictions and null results as well as positive findings, and it states which analyses are exploratory and which were pre-specified.

All data are publicly documented; two data sets permit research use but not redistribution, and scripts are provided to recreate the tasks from the original sources. All numbers in the text are generated by script from the analysis outputs. The use of AI-based assistants is declared in the Methods section, as the journal's policy requires.
