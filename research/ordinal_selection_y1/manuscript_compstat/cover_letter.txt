Dear Editors,

We submit the manuscript "Which metric should choose the model? Selection criteria, class weighting and endpoint recall in tabular ordinal classification" for consideration as an original research article in Computational Statistics.

Ordinal classifiers are usually tuned by maximizing a single validation criterion, most often the quadratically weighted kappa. The paper asks what that choice does to the extreme categories. On one fixed pool of proportional-odds and random-forest candidates, trained with and without class-balanced weights, seven selection criteria choose from the same saved inner cross-validation predictions. The study covers ten real tabular ordinal tasks from nine sources and four control tasks, all under grouped nested cross-validation, and a controlled simulation complements the real-data analysis. The main findings are:

- The selection criterion largely determines whether class weighting is adopted. Fixed-hyperparameter controls explain why: weighting nearly always increases MAE and reduces the worst class-wise error.
- The effect of class weighting on an endpoint category follows a simple empirical rule: it helps endpoints whose share is below 1/K and tends to hurt those above it. A short proposition proves the corresponding monotonicity of prior correction for the rarest and the most frequent category; for the other categories the comparison with 1/K is a heuristic. Selection by average class-wise MAE (AMAE) followed the same rule on the original tasks, which explains why it protected the endpoints in some tasks and harmed them in others.
- A constant middle-category prediction attains a data-independent normalized AMAE, ⌊K²/4⌋/{K(K−1)}, that is (K+1)/(4K) for odd K and K/{4(K−1)} for even K, with zero endpoint recall. In four of the ten tasks this level was lower than the AMAE of every fitted candidate.
- An explicit endpoint-recall floor added to the kappa criterion protected the endpoints in almost every cell in which it could be met on the inner predictions, although meeting it there is no guarantee for new data. No criterion was close to optimal on all evaluation metrics.
- Two confirmatory stages tested these claims. The first was pre-specified, with 24 candidates from three model families and a simulation designed for the 1/K rule. The second was pre-registered in the public repository of the study (commit 66e426e) before any fit, with four new data sources, an ordinal (Frank-Hall) model family and median decoding. 14 of the 18 predictions were supported. The failures are reported in full: the weighting rule was not confirmed within the ordinal family, the AMAE rule did not transfer to new sources, and an infeasible recall floor is only as good as its fallback.

We believe the work fits the journal's interest in computational methodology for statistical learning and in the assessment of statistical procedures. It continues a line of work published in the journal on how the evaluation metric changes model selection (de la Cruz Huayanay, Bazán and Russo, 2025, https://doi.org/10.1007/s00180-024-01539-5). The paper reports reversals, failed predictions and null results as well as positive findings, states which analyses are exploratory and which were pre-specified, and treats data sources, not tasks or model families, as the independent units in cross-task tests.

All data are publicly documented; two data sets permit research use but not redistribution, and scripts are provided to recreate the tasks from the original sources. All numbers in the text are generated by script from the analysis outputs. The figures were produced with Matplotlib 3.11 and are supplied as vector PDF files. The use of AI-based assistants is declared in Section 3.8 (Implementation, verification and use of AI tools), as the journal's policy requires.

This manuscript has not been published and is not under consideration elsewhere. Both authors have approved the submission, contributed equally to the work and share first authorship. The authors declare no competing interests.
