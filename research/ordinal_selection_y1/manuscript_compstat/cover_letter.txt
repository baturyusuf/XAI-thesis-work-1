Dear Editors,

We submit the manuscript "Which metric should choose the model? Selection criteria, class weighting and endpoint recall in tabular ordinal classification" for consideration as an original research article in Computational Statistics.

Ordinal classifiers are usually tuned by maximizing a single validation criterion, most often the quadratically weighted kappa. The paper asks what that choice does to the extreme categories. On one fixed pool of proportional-odds and random-forest candidates, trained with and without class-balanced weights, seven selection criteria choose from the same saved inner cross-validation predictions on six real tabular ordinal tasks under grouped nested cross-validation. A controlled simulation complements the real-data analysis. The main findings are:

- The selection criterion largely determines whether class weighting is adopted. Fixed-hyperparameter controls explain why: weighting always increases MAE and almost always reduces the worst class-wise error.
- No criterion is close to optimal on all evaluation metrics, as a cross-criterion regret analysis shows.
- A constant middle-category prediction attains a data-independent normalized AMAE of (K+1)/(4K) with zero endpoint recall. In three low-signal tasks this level was lower than the AMAE of every fitted candidate.
- MMAE and an explicit endpoint-recall floor protected the endpoint categories more consistently than AMAE, at a small cost in kappa.

We believe the work fits the journal's interest in computational methodology for statistical learning and in simulation-based assessment of statistical procedures. It continues a line of work published in the journal on how the evaluation metric changes model selection (de la Cruz Huayanay, Bazán and Russo, 2024, https://doi.org/10.1007/s00180-024-01539-5). The paper reports reversals and null results as well as positive findings, and it states explicitly which analyses are exploratory.

All data are publicly documented. Two data sets permit research use but not redistribution, and scripts are provided to recreate them from the original sources. The use of AI-based assistants is declared in the Methods section, as the journal's policy requires.

This manuscript has not been published and is not under consideration elsewhere. All authors have approved the submission. [Confirm: the authors declare no competing interests.]
