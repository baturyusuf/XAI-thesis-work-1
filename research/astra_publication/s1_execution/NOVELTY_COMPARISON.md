## Short positioning text

The study contributes a scoped empirical audit of ordinal employee-rating conclusions under alternative selection objectives and declared information policies. Established prediction, calibration and explanation methods are linked through identifiable held-out evidence. This differs in research question from an illustrative leakage-risk detector framework and a multi-dataset boosting/deep-learning benchmark; it does not claim a new algorithm, detector superiority, or the absence of comparable components in those works. The comparison is bounded by abstract-level access.

## Editorial implication

Positioning is stronger when based on the specific ordinal/policy findings rather than a list of combined audit tools. The central risk remains empirical scope and novelty sufficiency, not absence of a newly invented algorithm. No quantitative acceptance probability is inferred from these publications.
