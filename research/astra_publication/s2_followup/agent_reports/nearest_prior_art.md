# S2 nearest prior art: executable evidence-contract audit

**Purpose.** Independent literature map for Astra’s comparison of S2 with S1. S1 is already an integrated leakage-aware ordinal-HR ML audit with nested out-of-fold evaluation, calibration, exact-model SHAP, and proxy diagnostics. S2 is framed here as adding *testable utility* from an executable evidence-contract audit: detection of model/fold/policy mismatches and sensitivity of ordinal decisions. The question is not whether any component is new; it is whether the integrated contract detects consequential mismatches reliably, and changes decisions/usefully informs conclusions beyond S1.

**Scope and confidence.** Primary papers and official documentation below were checked through publisher/arXiv/library documentation or official pages where available. REFORMS' published identifier was provided and independently confirmed here against its official page and publisher citation: **10.1126/sciadv.adk3452**. Documentation establishes intended functionality, not independent efficacy. This is a focused nearest-art scan, not a systematic review. No claim that prior art is absent, nor a final strategy recommendation.

## What appears standard versus the plausible empirical gap

**Standard / heavily anticipated.** (1) Scientific ML reporting/checklist requirements (REFORMS); (2) production ML testing rubrics and suites (ML Test Score); (3) declarative data assertions (GX) and automated model/data checks (Deepchecks); (4) leakage identification/avoidance; (5) cross-validation/nested evaluation and correct scoring/threshold selection; (6) metamorphic/negative-control testing; and (7) testing explanation sensitivity. S2 is not distinguished by simply combining these labels, generating a checklist, executing ordinary data-quality checks, or computing an ordinal metric.

**Narrow gap worth Astra adjudicating (not a novelty conclusion):** Is an explicitly specified, executable *evidence contract* that binds the claimed fitted model, held-out fold / OOF row provenance, calibration artifact, decision-policy version, and resulting ordinal prediction materially better at detecting consequential provenance/policy mismatches than (a) S1’s integrated audit and (b) a strong manual/checklist or conventional test-suite comparator? Could blinded, seeded mutations demonstrate this with known ground truth, and do detected mismatches alter ordinal decisions or downstream conclusions? The possible contribution is the *measured incremental utility and failure characterization* of the integrated contract, not an assumed new algorithm.

Potential mutations (make each ground-truth label and expected assertion explicit): substitute a model artifact while retaining claimed metadata; permute or reassign fold IDs/OOF rows; use in-fold predictions in a field labeled OOF; substitute calibration fit on a different fold/model; change ordinal class order/encoding; alter threshold/cost policy while retaining old report; mismatch probability columns with class labels; or mismatch SHAP estimator/output from the reported predictor. Include valid/no-op mutations as negative controls. Avoid deliberately leaking held-out information into the reference truth or tuning to the outcomes.

## Comparator and empirical design implications for Astra

A defensible empirical question is comparative, not “does the tool run?” Pre-register a fixed contract and a mutation catalogue before evaluating. Compare at least: (A) S1 as described (without adding the S2 contract), (B) S1 plus the executable contract, and (C) a transparent manual/REFORMS-style checklist or existing standard checks where feasible. An ML Test Score-inspired baseline may fit a production-readiness framing, but adaptation and coverage must be disclosed; it is not interchangeable with REFORMS. GX and Deepchecks are useful tool precedents, not automatically matched baselines for provenance assertions. State comparator scope to prevent straw-manning.

Use independently authored synthetic or suitably de-identified cases, ground-truth mutation labels hidden from the audit operator, and a held-out set of mutation scenarios not used to author/tune assertions. Separate development from evaluation by scenario family and, where applicable, dataset/subject—not merely shuffled mutation instances. Do not derive expected outcomes or thresholds from the final evaluation set. Report per-mutation and aggregate sensitivity/recall, specificity or false-alert rate on valid controls, precision, severity-weighted detection, and undetected high-consequence errors; uncertainty intervals should reflect clustering by base case/scenario. Record time/manual effort if workflow utility is claimed. A small toy test proves execution only, not utility.

For ordinal decision sensitivity, prespecify class order, policy/cost matrix and admissible thresholds without viewing final test labels. Quantify changes in chosen class/action and ordinal loss (e.g., absolute class-distance error), confusion/error distance, and policy-relevant consequences under each caught mismatch. Evaluate the same examples with and without the mismatch and contract intervention. Avoid conflating classification performance with policy utility; a detector can be accurate at flagging invalid artifacts yet have no decision value, or vice versa. Report both.

Keep train/test independence at two levels: (i) preserve nested OOF/calibration/exact-model SHAP conditions from S1; never use outer test outcomes to choose contract rules, calibrators, thresholds, or mutation severity; (ii) reserve mutation families/cases for final evaluation so contract design is not tuned on its benchmark. If evaluating a real HR dataset, respect its sampling/group/time structure in splitting, and perform any feature/model selection within training partitions. Explain that test labels are used only for final outcome scoring, not contract construction.

### Questions for Astra (not a recommendation)

1. Which exact claims does S1 already verify from executable artifacts, versus infer from reports/configuration? Without that boundary, “incremental over S1” is untestable.
2. Can the intended model/fold/policy identities be independently specified as a compact machine-readable contract, and can each be mutated while retaining plausible but inconsistent artifacts?
3. What comparator fairly represents the strongest existing workflow (REFORMS compliance plus ordinary tests, or a named tool), and what is out of scope for each?
4. Which ordinal decision outcomes matter, and what independently justified cost matrix / policy makes “sensitivity” meaningful?
5. Is the target contribution a benchmark of audit detection, a reproducible artifact/protocol, or applied HR findings? These entail different novelty and evidence claims.

## Citation integrity / limitations

REFORMS publication DOI and official checklist confirmed as above; its page explicitly describes a 32-item, 8-section reporting standard, including leakage, metrics/uncertainty, and computational reproducibility. The linked official documentation is descriptive product evidence, not peer-reviewed effectiveness evidence. Some publisher/official URLs were inaccessible in this search session; bibliographic identifiers in citations should be checked against institutional library metadata before submission. In particular, use the ML Test Score DOI and source metadata to confirm conference pagination/authors in the bibliography. No 2025–2026 independent efficacy study specifically testing this proposed cross-artifact contract was verified in this bounded scan; that is **not** evidence none exists. Expand with targeted database searches for “ML artifact provenance validation,” “pipeline lineage conformance,” “cross-validation leakage automated detection,” “ordinal decision policy audit,” and citation chaining before making any novelty statement.
